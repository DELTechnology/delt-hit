\phantomsection\subsection*{Supplementary Note 3: Enrichment-scoring methods}
\label{supp:statistical-methods}

The enrichment benchmark compares six scores that are commonly useful for DEL
selection data: DELT-Hit simple-count enrichment, DELT-Hit normalized z-score,
DELT-Hit edgeR log fold change, DELi Normalized Sequence Count (NSC), DELi MLE,
and DELi norm-z. This section defines the scores and the experimental settings
where each is most appropriate before the benchmark results are presented.

\textbf{Normalized z-score (Faver et al.\ formulation).}
Let $D$ denote the total number of distinct disynthon (or higher-cycle) compound
identities in the library and $n$ the total number of sequencing reads in a
selection. For a given compound, let $c$ be its raw read count. The observed
sequence frequency is $p_{\rm obs} = c / n$ and the expected null frequency under
uniform library representation is $p_i = 1/D$. The depth-insensitive z-score
is\cite{ref42}

\begin{equation}
z_n = \frac{p_{\rm obs} - p_i}{\sqrt{p_i\,(1 - p_i)}}.
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}

The closely related fold enrichment,
\begin{equation}
E = \frac{p_{\rm obs}}{p_i} = c \cdot \frac{D}{n},
\label{eq:fe}
\end{equation}
provides a scale-invariant measure of enrichment that is more interpretable for
hit-picking. The recommended primary hit threshold is $E \geq 2.0$; a fixed
$z_n$ cutoff is not recommended because practical score ranges depend on
library diversity, sampling sparsity, and selection context. We use this
normalized, depth-insensitive Faver formulation rather than the conventional
binomial z-score, whose numerical value increases with sequencing depth.

\textbf{Simple-count enrichment.}
The simple-count method averages raw sequencing counts across replicates within
each condition and scores each compound by the difference between the protein
and no-protein means, $\bar{c}_{\rm prot} - \bar{c}_{\rm ctrl}$. It is a
transparent count-based baseline that averages replicate counts and does not
assume a parametric count distribution. Its main weakness is sensitivity to
read-depth differences and raw-count scale, because the current default workflow
operates on raw counts rather than library-size-normalized frequencies. It is
therefore most informative when sequencing depths are comparable or as a simple
benchmark baseline.

\textbf{edgeR negative-binomial GLM.}
Compound-level read counts per selection are modelled as negative-binomial
(NB) random variables. The NB model accommodates overdispersion relative to
Poisson counts, which arises in DEL experiments from variation in sequencing
depth, barcode-decoding efficiency, and chemical synthesis yield. Per-compound
dispersion is estimated by empirical Bayes shrinkage towards a trend fitted
across all compounds\cite{ref36}. A likelihood-ratio test (LRT) is performed
for each compound to compare the NB-GLM fit under a two-group design
(protein vs.\ no-protein) against a null model; Benjamini--Hochberg
false-discovery rate (FDR) correction is applied across all compounds.
Compounds are ranked by log$_2$ fold change (logFC); FDR is available as a
secondary filter.

For DEL hit picking, the main limitation is not that edgeR is statistically
inappropriate, but that its objective differs from primary rank ordering.
Typical compound-level DEL count tables remain sparse across hundreds of
thousands of library members even with replicate selections. Low-count
positives near the detection limit can therefore receive conservative logFC
estimates or weaker statistical support, whereas frequency-based scores rank
them directly by observed selection enrichment. For this reason, edgeR is best
used to annotate a shortlist and flag statistically supported contrasts, rather
than as the sole full-library ranking signal.

\textbf{DELi NSC and equivalence with z-score.}
The Normalized Sequence Count used in \deli{}\cite{ref41} is the observed
selection frequency, $\mathrm{NSC} = c/n = p_{\rm obs}$. For a uniform library,
where every compound has the same expected null probability, NSC and the
normalized z-score produce the same rank ordering. This makes NSC the closest
\deli{} comparator to the \delthit{} z-score in the NF2 benchmark.

\textbf{DELi MLE.}
The DELi maximum-likelihood estimate uses a Poisson count model to estimate
enrichment relative to the no-protein baseline. This makes it conceptually close
to ratio-style enrichment over a measured control. It can be informative when
control counts are well sampled, but it is sensitive to zero or near-zero
control counts because small denominator changes can move low-count compounds
substantially in the ranked list.

\textbf{DELi norm-z.}
The DELi normalized z-score compares post-selection counts with the empirical
mean and standard deviation of the no-protein controls. This denominator-based
normalization is most useful when control counts have enough variation to
provide a stable standard deviation. In sparse no-protein-control settings with
many zero or near-zero control counts, the denominator can become unstable;
therefore norm-z should be interpreted cautiously for sparse DEL count tables.
